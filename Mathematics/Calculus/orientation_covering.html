Orientability and Covering Maps

Proving that a manifold is orientable is usually a matter of exhibiting an orientation, but proving that a manifold is not orientable is harder, because one must rule out every possible orientation at once. The theory of covering spaces provides one of the most effective tools for the negative direction, by tying orientability to the behavior of deck transformations. The first observation is that orientability always lifts along a cover.

Proposition (Orientability Lifts Along Covers)

If \(\pi : E \to M\) is a smooth covering map between smooth manifolds with or without boundary and \(M\) is orientable, then \(E\) is also orientable.

Proof.

A covering map is a local diffeomorphism, so an orientation of \(M\) pulls back to an orientation of \(E\). Hence \(E\) is orientable.

The converse direction, descending an orientation from \(E\) to \(M\), is the interesting one, and it fails in general. It succeeds exactly when the symmetries of the cover respect the orientation chosen upstairs. To state this precisely we name the relevant condition on a group action.

Definition (Orientation-Preserving Action)

Suppose a Lie group \(G\) acts smoothly on an oriented smooth manifold \(E\), with or without boundary, say on the left. The action is an orientation-preserving action if for each \(g \in G\) the diffeomorphism \(x \mapsto g \cdot x\) is orientation-preserving.

For a normal covering the deck group acts transitively on each fiber, and this transitivity is exactly what allows a fiberwise condition to assemble into a global orientation downstairs. The result is a clean criterion. The base is orientable precisely when the deck group preserves the orientation of the total space.

Theorem (Orientability of the Base of a Normal Cover)

Suppose \(E\) is a connected, oriented, smooth manifold, with or without boundary, and \(\pi : E \to M\) is a smooth normal covering map. Then \(M\) is orientable if and only if the action of \(\mathrm{Aut}_\pi(E)\) on \(E\) is orientation-preserving.

Proof.

Let \(\mathcal{O}_E\) denote the given orientation on \(E\).

Suppose first that \(M\) is orientable, and let \(q\) be an arbitrary point of \(E\). Because \(M\), the image of the connected manifold \(E\) under the surjection \(\pi\), is connected and orientable, it has exactly two orientations, and one of them, call it \(\mathcal{O}_M\), has the property that \(d\pi_q : T_q E \to T_{\pi(q)} M\) is orientation-preserving. The pullback orientation \(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_M\) then agrees with the given orientation at \(q\), so by connectedness it equals \(\mathcal{O}_E\) everywhere. Now let \(\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}_\pi(E)\). The identity \(\pi \circ \varphi = \pi\) gives \[ \varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}_E = \varphi^{*}\bigl(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_M\bigr) = (\pi \circ \varphi)^{*}\mathcal{O}_M = \pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_M = \mathcal{O}_E, \] so \(\varphi\) is orientation-preserving. Hence the action of \(\mathrm{Aut}_\pi(E)\) preserves orientation.

Conversely, suppose the action of \(\mathrm{Aut}_\pi(E)\) is orientation-preserving, and let \(p \in M\). Choose a connected evenly covered neighborhood \(U\) of \(p\). There is a smooth local section \(\sigma : U \to E\), which induces the pullback orientation \(\sigma^{*}\mathcal{O}_E\) on \(U\). Suppose \(\sigma_1 : U \to E\) is any other smooth local section over \(U\). Because \(\pi\) is a normal cover, \(\mathrm{Aut}_\pi(E)\) acts transitively on each fiber, so there is a deck transformation \(\varphi\) with \(\sigma_1(p) = \varphi\bigl(\sigma(p)\bigr)\). Then \(\varphi \circ \sigma\) is a local section agreeing with \(\sigma_1\) at \(p\). Both are lifts of the inclusion \(U \hookrightarrow M\), and \(U\) is connected, so unique lifting gives \(\sigma_1 = \varphi \circ \sigma\) on all of \(U\). Because \(\varphi\) is orientation-preserving, \[ \sigma_1^{*}\mathcal{O}_E = \sigma^{*}\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}_E = \sigma^{*}\mathcal{O}_E, \] so the orientations induced by \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma_1\) coincide.

We may therefore define an orientation \(\mathcal{O}_M\) on \(M\) by declaring it on each evenly covered open set to be the pullback induced by any local section. If two such sets \(U\) and \(U'\) meet, each point of \(U \cap U'\) has a connected open neighborhood \(W \subseteq U \cap U'\), because \(M\) is locally path-connected. The set \(W\) is again evenly covered, since each sheet over \(U\) meets \(\pi^{-1}(W)\) in a connected set mapped homeomorphically onto \(W\). Local sections over \(U\) and over \(U'\) restrict to local sections over \(W\), so the computation just made shows that the two orientations agree on \(W\). Hence \(\mathcal{O}_M\) is a well-defined orientation, and \(M\) is orientable.

The theorem converts a question about the global topology of \(M\) into a question about the signs of finitely many diffeomorphisms of \(E\), which is often decidable by direct computation. The two examples that follow carry it out in the cases that recur most often in applications.

Projective Spaces and the Möbius Band

Real projective space is the quotient of a sphere by the antipodal identification, and the quotient map is the prototypical normal cover. Whether the projective space is orientable therefore reduces, through the criterion just proved, to whether the antipodal map preserves the orientation of the sphere. The answer alternates with dimension.

Proposition (Orientability of Projective Spaces)

For \(n \geq 1\), real projective space \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) is orientable if and only if \(n\) is odd.

Proof.

The quotient map \(q : S^n \to \mathbb{RP}^n\) identifying antipodal points is a two-sheeted smooth covering map, one of the model examples of smooth coverings. The antipodal map \(\alpha(x) = -x\) is a diffeomorphism of \(S^n\) with \(q \circ \alpha = q\), so it is a deck transformation. Conversely, a deck transformation \(\varphi\) sends a chosen point \(x_0\) to \(x_0\) or to \(-x_0\), and \(\varphi\), the identity, and \(\alpha\) are all lifts of \(q\) through \(q\). Since \(S^n\) is connected for \(n \geq 1\), unique lifting gives \(\varphi = \mathrm{Id}\) or \(\varphi = \alpha\). Thus \(\mathrm{Aut}_q(S^n) = \{\mathrm{Id}, \alpha\}\), which acts transitively on each fiber \(\{x, -x\}\), so \(q\) is a normal covering. Give \(S^n\) its standard orientation, determined by the outward unit normal \(N_x = x\). An ordered basis \((E_1, \dots, E_n)\) of \(T_x S^n\) is positively oriented exactly when \((x, E_1, \dots, E_n)\) is positively oriented in \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\). The antipodal map has differential \(-\,\mathrm{Id}\) on \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\), so it sends such a basis to \((-E_1, \dots, -E_n)\) at the point \(-x\), where the outward normal is \(N_{-x} = -x\). Comparing orientations, \[ \bigl(N_{-x}, -E_1, \dots, -E_n\bigr) = \bigl(-x, -E_1, \dots, -E_n\bigr) = (-1)^{n+1}\bigl(x, E_1, \dots, E_n\bigr) \] as a relation between signed bases of \(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\), since negating all \(n+1\) vectors contributes the factor \((-1)^{n+1}\).

Thus \(\alpha\) carries the induced orientation of \(T_x S^n\) to \((-1)^{n+1}\) times the induced orientation of \(T_{-x} S^n\), so \(\alpha\) is orientation-preserving precisely when \((-1)^{n+1} = 1\), that is, when \(n\) is odd. By the criterion for the base of a normal cover, \(\mathbb{RP}^n\) is orientable if and only if the deck action is orientation-preserving, which happens if and only if \(n\) is odd.

The case \(n = 2\) is the one that surfaces in applications. Here \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is nonorientable, and its orientation double cover is the two-sphere, with the antipodal map as the nontrivial deck transformation. A signal defined on directions in three-dimensional space, invariant under reversal of a director, lives naturally on \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) rather than on \(S^2\). This is precisely the domain of the diffusion signal in diffusion-weighted magnetic resonance imaging, where the measurement at each voxel depends only on an unoriented axis.

One way to bring orientation-dependent machinery to bear is to pass to the orientable cover \(S^2\). Another, pursued in gauge-equivariant networks for such data, is to work directly on the nonorientable \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) using a gauge formulation that does not presuppose a global orientation. Either way the orientation double cover is the structure that organizes the choices, and the covering theorem of the next section guarantees it exists and is essentially unique.

The same mechanism diagnoses the most familiar nonorientable surface directly, without reference to a sphere.

Proposition (Nonorientability of the Möbius Band)

The Möbius band is not orientable.

Proof.

Realize the open Möbius band as the quotient of \(\mathbb{R}^2\) by the smooth, free, and properly discontinuous action of \(\mathbb{Z}\) given by \[ n \cdot (x, y) = \bigl(x + n,\, (-1)^n y\bigr). \] The quotient map \(q : \mathbb{R}^2 \to E\) onto the quotient \(E\) is a smooth normal covering, and the deck group is the image of this \(\mathbb{Z}\)-action. We take for granted here that the quotient of a connected smooth manifold, with or without boundary, by a smooth, free, and properly discontinuous action of a discrete group is a smooth manifold and that the quotient map is a smooth normal covering whose deck group is the acting group. This also covers the restriction of \(q\) to the strip \(\mathbb{R} \times [-r, r]\) used below.

Give \(\mathbb{R}^2\) its standard orientation, with orientation form \(dx \wedge dy\). The generator \((x, y) \mapsto (x + 1, -y)\) pulls this form back to \(dx \wedge d(-y) = -\,dx \wedge dy\), so it reverses orientation. The deck action is therefore not orientation-preserving, and by the criterion for the base of a normal cover, \(E\) is not orientable. The closed Möbius band, being the image of a strip \(\mathbb{R} \times [-r, r]\) under the same covering, fails to be orientable by the identical argument applied to the restricted cover.

These computations exhibit nonorientability concretely, but they presuppose a convenient covering in each case. The final section removes that dependence by constructing, for every manifold, a canonical orientable cover whose connectedness encodes orientability.

The Orientation Covering

Every smooth manifold, orientable or not, admits a canonical two-sheeted cover that is itself orientable, built so that the fiber over a point records the two orientations of the tangent space there. To treat the orientable and nonorientable cases uniformly, it is convenient to allow the cover to be disconnected, and we relax the definition of a covering map accordingly.

Definition (Generalized Covering Map)

Let \(N\) and \(M\) be topological spaces. A map \(\pi : N \to M\) is a generalized covering map if \(N\) is locally path-connected, \(\pi\) is continuous and surjective, and each point of \(M\) has an open neighborhood \(U\) that is evenly covered, meaning that each component of \(\pi^{-1}(U)\) is mapped homeomorphically onto \(U\) by \(\pi\). This differs from the definition of a covering map only in that connectedness is required of neither space and local path-connectedness is required only of \(N\). If in addition \(N\) and \(M\) are smooth manifolds with or without boundary and \(\pi\) is a local diffeomorphism, then \(\pi\) is a generalized smooth covering map.

Discarding connectedness costs nothing locally, and over a connected base it can be recovered one component at a time.

Lemma (Components of a Generalized Covering)

Let \(\pi : N \to M\) be a generalized covering map, and suppose \(M\) is connected. Then the restriction of \(\pi\) to each component of \(N\) is a covering map.

Proof.

Let \(W\) be a component of \(N\). Because \(N\) is locally path-connected, \(W\) is open in \(N\), and as an open subset it is itself locally path-connected. The base \(M\) is also locally path-connected, because every point of \(M\) has an evenly covered neighborhood \(U\), and \(U\) is homeomorphic to a sheet over it, which is a component of the locally path-connected open set \(\pi^{-1}(U)\) and hence an open, locally path-connected subset of \(N\). Thus \(M\), connected by hypothesis, is also locally path-connected, as the definition of a covering map requires of its base.

Let \(U \subseteq M\) be evenly covered. Each component of \(\pi^{-1}(U)\) is connected and therefore lies in a single component of \(N\). Hence \((\pi|_W)^{-1}(U) = \pi^{-1}(U) \cap W\) is either empty or a nonempty union of components of \(\pi^{-1}(U)\), each mapped homeomorphically onto \(U\). In particular, every point of \(\pi(W)\) has a neighborhood that is evenly covered by \(\pi|_W\).

It remains to show that \(\pi|_W\) is surjective. Each point of \(N\) lies in an open sheet that \(\pi\) maps homeomorphically onto an open subset of \(M\), so \(\pi\) is an open map and \(\pi(W)\) is open. If \(p \in M \setminus \pi(W)\) and \(U\) is an evenly covered neighborhood of \(p\), then \(\pi^{-1}(U) \cap W = \varnothing\), since otherwise it would contain a whole sheet over \(U\) and hence a point over \(p\). Thus \(U \subseteq M \setminus \pi(W)\), and \(\pi(W)\) is closed. Since \(W\) is nonempty and \(M\) is connected, \(\pi(W) = M\).

The candidate for the canonical cover is the set of all orientations of all tangent spaces.

Definition (The Orientation Covering)

Let \(M\) be a connected smooth positive-dimensional manifold, with or without boundary, and let \[ \widehat M = \bigl\{ (p, \mathcal{O}_p) : p \in M,\ \mathcal{O}_p \text{ an orientation of } T_p M \bigr\}. \] The projection \(\widehat\pi : \widehat M \to M\) sending \((p, \mathcal{O}_p)\) to \(p\) has every fiber of cardinality two, since each tangent space has exactly two orientations. The map \(\widehat\pi\) is called the orientation covering of \(M\).

The set \(\widehat M\) carries no structure yet. The next proposition supplies a topology, a smooth structure, and an orientation, all read off from the orientations of open subsets of \(M\).

Proposition (Properties of the Orientation Covering)

Let \(M\) be a connected smooth positive-dimensional manifold, with or without boundary, and let \(\widehat\pi : \widehat M \to M\) be its orientation covering. Then \(\widehat M\) can be given the structure of a smooth manifold with or without boundary, oriented so that \(d\widehat\pi_{(p, \mathcal{O}_p)}\) carries the orientation of \(T_{(p, \mathcal{O}_p)}\widehat M\) to \(\mathcal{O}_p\), with the following properties.

(a) \(\widehat\pi\) is a generalized smooth covering map.

(b) A connected open subset \(U \subseteq M\) is evenly covered by \(\widehat\pi\) if and only if it is orientable.

(c) If \(U \subseteq M\) is open and \(\mathcal{O}\) is an orientation of \(U\), then \(p \mapsto (p, \mathcal{O}_p)\) is a smooth local section of \(\widehat\pi\) over \(U\), and it pulls the orientation of \(\widehat M\) back to \(\mathcal{O}\). In particular, every orientation of an evenly covered open subset of \(M\) is the pullback orientation induced by a local section.

With this structure, \(\widehat M\) is called the oriented double covering of \(M\).

Proof.

We first topologize \(\widehat M\). For each open \(U \subseteq M\) and each orientation \(\mathcal{O}\) of \(U\), let \[ \widehat U_{\mathcal{O}} = \bigl\{ (p, \mathcal{O}_p) : p \in U \bigr\}, \] where \(\mathcal{O}_p\) is the orientation of \(T_pM\) determined by \(\mathcal{O}\). Every point of \(M\) has an orientable open neighborhood. The domain of a smooth chart \((U, \varphi)\) is oriented by pulling back the standard orientation of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) or \(\mathbb{H}^n\) through \(\varphi\), and an open subset of an oriented open set inherits the restricted orientation. Given \((p, \mathcal{O}_p) \in \widehat M\), choose such a \(U\) with an orientation \(\mathcal{O}\), replaced by its opposite if necessary so that it determines \(\mathcal{O}_p\) at \(p\). Then \((p, \mathcal{O}_p) \in \widehat U_{\mathcal{O}}\), so these sets cover \(\widehat M\). If \((p, \mathcal{O}_p)\) lies in \(\widehat U_{\mathcal{O}} \cap \widehat{U'}_{\mathcal{O}'}\), let \(V\) be the component of \(U \cap U'\) containing \(p\), which is open because \(M\) is locally path-connected. The restrictions \(\mathcal{O}|_V\) and \(\mathcal{O}'|_V\) agree at \(p\), hence agree on all of \(V\), so \(\widehat V_{\mathcal{O}|_V}\) is contained in the intersection and contains \((p, \mathcal{O}_p)\). The sets \(\widehat U_{\mathcal{O}}\) therefore form a basis for a topology on \(\widehat M\).

The map \(\widehat\pi\) carries each basis set \(\widehat U_{\mathcal{O}}\) bijectively onto \(U\). It is continuous, because for open \(V \subseteq M\) the set \(\widehat\pi^{-1}(V) \cap \widehat U_{\mathcal{O}}\) is the basis set \(\widehat{(U \cap V)}_{\mathcal{O}|_{U \cap V}}\), so \(\widehat\pi^{-1}(V)\) is a union of basis sets. It is open, because it carries each basis set onto an open subset of \(M\). Hence each restriction \(\widehat U_{\mathcal{O}} \to U\) is a continuous open bijection, that is, a homeomorphism, and \(\widehat M\) is locally homeomorphic to \(M\), in particular locally path-connected.

Now let \(U \subseteq M\) be a connected orientable open set with an orientation \(\mathcal{O}\). The fiber over each \(p \in U\) consists of the orientation determined by \(\mathcal{O}\) and its opposite, so \(\widehat\pi^{-1}(U) = \widehat U_{\mathcal{O}} \sqcup \widehat U_{-\mathcal{O}}\), a disjoint union of two open sets each mapped homeomorphically onto \(U\) and hence connected. These two sets are therefore the components of \(\widehat\pi^{-1}(U)\), and \(U\) is evenly covered. Every point of \(M\) has such a neighborhood, for instance the preimage under a chart of a small ball or half-ball, so \(\widehat\pi\) is a generalized covering map. By the lemma on components, its restriction to each component \(W\) of \(\widehat M\) is a covering map onto the connected smooth manifold \(M\). By the propositions on covering spaces of smooth manifolds without and with boundary, each \(W\) is a topological manifold, with boundary if \(M\) has one, and carries a unique smooth structure making \(\widehat\pi|_W\) a smooth covering map. The components are open, and each maps onto \(M\), so each contains a point of every two-point fiber and there are at most two of them. Hence \(\widehat M\) is Hausdorff and second-countable, and the smooth structures on the components combine into one on \(\widehat M\) for which \(\widehat\pi\) is a local diffeomorphism. This proves (a).

Next we orient \(\widehat M\). At \(\hat p = (p, \mathcal{O}_p)\) the differential \(d\widehat\pi_{\hat p}\) is an isomorphism, and we give \(T_{\hat p}\widehat M\) the unique orientation \(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\hat p}\) that it carries to \(\mathcal{O}_p\). On each basis set \(\widehat U_{\mathcal{O}}\), this pointwise orientation is the one that makes \(\widehat\pi|_{\widehat U_{\mathcal{O}}}\) orientation-preserving onto \((U, \mathcal{O})\), which is the orientation pulled back from \((U, \mathcal{O})\) by this local diffeomorphism. It is therefore continuous, and \(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}\) is an orientation of \(\widehat M\).

For (b), every connected orientable open set was shown above to be evenly covered. Conversely, let \(U \subseteq M\) be a connected open set evenly covered by \(\widehat\pi\), and let \(S\) be a component of \(\widehat\pi^{-1}(U)\), which is nonempty by surjectivity. Then \(S\) is open, being a component of an open subset of the locally path-connected space \(\widehat M\), and \(\widehat\pi|_S\) is a bijective local diffeomorphism onto \(U\), hence a diffeomorphism. Its inverse \(\sigma : U \to \widehat M\) is a smooth local section and a local diffeomorphism, so \(\sigma^*\widehat{\mathcal{O}}\) is an orientation of \(U\).

For (c), let \(\mathcal{O}\) be an orientation of an open set \(U \subseteq M\) and put \(\sigma(p) = (p, \mathcal{O}_p)\). Then \(\sigma\) is the inverse of the map \(\widehat\pi|_{\widehat U_{\mathcal{O}}} : \widehat U_{\mathcal{O}} \to U\), which is a bijective local diffeomorphism and hence a diffeomorphism, so \(\sigma\) is smooth. Since \(d\widehat\pi_{\sigma(p)} \circ d\sigma_p\) is the identity and \(d\widehat\pi_{\sigma(p)}\) carries \(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\sigma(p)}\) to \(\mathcal{O}_p\), the differential \(d\sigma_p\) carries \(\mathcal{O}_p\) to \(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\sigma(p)}\), which says \(\sigma^*\widehat{\mathcal{O}} = \mathcal{O}\). An evenly covered open subset of \(M\) with an orientation is a special case.

The defining feature of \(\widehat M\) is that orientations of \(M\) correspond to global sections of \(\widehat\pi\). Whether such a section exists is a question about how the two sheets are connected, and that is precisely what the next theorem resolves. Orientability of \(M\) is the same as disconnectedness of \(\widehat M\).

Theorem (Orientation Covering Theorem)

Let \(M\) be a connected smooth positive-dimensional manifold, with or without boundary, and let \(\widehat\pi : \widehat M \to M\) be its orientation covering.

If \(M\) is orientable, then \(\widehat M\) has exactly two components, and the restriction of \(\widehat\pi\) to each component is a diffeomorphism onto \(M\). If \(M\) is nonorientable, then \(\widehat M\) is connected, and \(\widehat\pi\) is a two-sheeted smooth covering map.

Proof.

If \(M\) is orientable, let \(\mathcal{O}\) be an orientation of \(M\). Since \(M\) is connected, part (b) of the properties of the orientation covering shows that \(M\) itself is evenly covered, and its proof gives \(\widehat M = \widehat M_{\mathcal{O}} \sqcup \widehat M_{-\mathcal{O}}\), the basis sets for \(U = M\), each homeomorphic to \(M\) and hence connected. Each of these two components is mapped by \(\widehat\pi\) bijectively onto \(M\) as a local diffeomorphism, hence diffeomorphically, and the two components correspond to the two orientations of \(M\).

Now suppose \(M\) is nonorientable. Let \(W\) be a component of \(\widehat M\). By the lemma on components, \(\widehat\pi|_W\) is a covering map onto \(M\), and since it is a local diffeomorphism it is a smooth covering map. Its fibers all have the same cardinality, which is \(1\) or \(2\) because \(W\) is nonempty and the fibers of \(\widehat\pi\) have two points. If the cardinality were \(1\), then \(\widehat\pi|_W\) would be an injective smooth covering map, hence a diffeomorphism, and its inverse would be a smooth global section \(\sigma\) of \(\widehat\pi\). The pullback \(\sigma^*\widehat{\mathcal{O}}\) would then be an orientation of \(M\), contradicting nonorientability.

Therefore the cardinality is \(2\), so \(W\) contains every point of \(\widehat M\) and \(W = \widehat M\). Thus \(\widehat M\) is connected, \(\widehat\pi\) is a covering map by the same lemma, and being a local diffeomorphism it is a two-sheeted smooth covering map.

The orientation covering is determined by \(M\) up to isomorphism, independent of the particular construction used to build it.

Theorem (Uniqueness of the Orientation Covering)

Let \(M\) be a nonorientable connected smooth manifold, with or without boundary, and let \(\widehat\pi : \widehat M \to M\) be its orientation covering. If \(\widetilde M\) is an oriented smooth manifold admitting a two-sheeted smooth covering map \(\widetilde\pi : \widetilde M \to M\), then there is a unique orientation-preserving diffeomorphism \(\varphi : \widetilde M \to \widehat M\) such that \(\widehat\pi \circ \varphi = \widetilde\pi\).

Proof.

Write \(\mathcal{O}^{\widetilde M}\) for the orientation of \(\widetilde M\), and define \(\varphi : \widetilde M \to \widehat M\) by \[ \varphi(\tilde q) = \bigl(\widetilde\pi(\tilde q),\ d\widetilde\pi_{\tilde q}(\mathcal{O}^{\widetilde M}_{\tilde q})\bigr), \] where the differential \(d\widetilde\pi_{\tilde q}\), an isomorphism because \(\widetilde\pi\) is a local diffeomorphism, carries the orientation of \(T_{\tilde q}\widetilde M\) to an orientation of \(T_{\widetilde\pi(\tilde q)}M\). By construction \(\widehat\pi \circ \varphi = \widetilde\pi\).

To see that \(\varphi\) is smooth, fix \(\tilde q \in \widetilde M\), let \(U\) be an open set evenly covered by \(\widetilde\pi\) that contains \(\widetilde\pi(\tilde q)\), and let \(\widetilde S\) be the sheet over \(U\) containing \(\tilde q\), with inverse diffeomorphism \(s = (\widetilde\pi|_{\widetilde S})^{-1} : U \to \widetilde S\). The pullback \(\mathcal{O}^U = s^*\mathcal{O}^{\widetilde M}\) is an orientation of \(U\), and for \(\tilde x \in \widetilde S\) the differential \(d\widetilde\pi_{\tilde x} = (ds_{\widetilde\pi(\tilde x)})^{-1}\) carries \(\mathcal{O}^{\widetilde M}_{\tilde x}\) to \(\mathcal{O}^U_{\widetilde\pi(\tilde x)}\). Let \(\sigma : U \to \widehat M\), \(\sigma(p) = (p, \mathcal{O}^U_p)\), be the smooth local section of part (c) of the properties of the orientation covering. Then \(\varphi = \sigma \circ \widetilde\pi\) on \(\widetilde S\). Since \(\widetilde\pi|_{\widetilde S}\) is a diffeomorphism onto \(U\) and \(\sigma\) is a diffeomorphism onto the open set \(\widehat U_{\mathcal{O}^U}\), the map \(\varphi\) restricts to a diffeomorphism of \(\widetilde S\) onto an open subset of \(\widehat M\). Hence \(\varphi\) is smooth and a local diffeomorphism.

Next we show that \(\varphi\) is bijective. Let \(A\) be the set of \(p \in M\) over which \(\varphi\) is injective on the two-point fiber \(\widetilde\pi^{-1}(p)\). Let \(U\) be evenly covered by \(\widetilde\pi\). It is connected, being homeomorphic to a sheet, and it has exactly two sheets \(\widetilde S_1\) and \(\widetilde S_2\). Let \(\mathcal{O}_1\) and \(\mathcal{O}_2\) be the orientations of \(U\) they induce as in the preceding paragraph, so that for each \(p \in U\) the two points of \(\widetilde\pi^{-1}(p)\) are sent to \((p, (\mathcal{O}_1)_p)\) and \((p, (\mathcal{O}_2)_p)\). Because \(U\) is connected, \(\mathcal{O}_2\) equals either \(\mathcal{O}_1\) or \(-\mathcal{O}_1\) on all of \(U\). Hence either \(U \subseteq A\) or \(U \cap A = \varnothing\). The sets \(A\) and \(M \setminus A\) are therefore open, and since \(M\) is connected, \(A = M\) or \(A = \varnothing\).

Suppose \(A = \varnothing\). Then over each evenly covered \(U\) as above, \(\varphi(\widetilde M) \cap \widehat\pi^{-1}(U) = \varphi\bigl(\widetilde\pi^{-1}(U)\bigr) = \widehat U_{\mathcal{O}_1}\), and the complement of \(\varphi(\widetilde M)\) in \(\widehat\pi^{-1}(U)\) is \(\widehat U_{-\mathcal{O}_1}\). Since these sets \(U\) cover \(M\), both \(\varphi(\widetilde M)\) and its complement are open in \(\widehat M\), and both are nonempty, the complement because it meets every fiber. This contradicts the connectedness of \(\widehat M\), which holds by the Orientation Covering Theorem because \(M\) is nonorientable. Therefore \(A = M\). Each two-point fiber of \(\widetilde\pi\) is then mapped injectively, hence bijectively, onto the two-point fiber of \(\widehat\pi\) over the same point. Thus \(\varphi\) is bijective, and a bijective local diffeomorphism is a diffeomorphism.

Finally, \(\varphi\) is orientation-preserving. The identity \(\widehat\pi \circ \varphi = \widetilde\pi\) gives \(d\widehat\pi_{\varphi(\tilde q)} \circ d\varphi_{\tilde q} = d\widetilde\pi_{\tilde q}\), so \(d\widehat\pi_{\varphi(\tilde q)}\) carries \(d\varphi_{\tilde q}(\mathcal{O}^{\widetilde M}_{\tilde q})\) to \(d\widetilde\pi_{\tilde q}(\mathcal{O}^{\widetilde M}_{\tilde q})\), the second coordinate of \(\varphi(\tilde q)\). By the definition of the orientation of \(\widehat M\), this says that \(d\varphi_{\tilde q}\) carries \(\mathcal{O}^{\widetilde M}_{\tilde q}\) to the orientation of \(T_{\varphi(\tilde q)}\widehat M\). For uniqueness, let \(\varphi'\) be another orientation-preserving diffeomorphism with \(\widehat\pi \circ \varphi' = \widetilde\pi\). The same computation shows that \(\varphi'(\tilde q)\) has first coordinate \(\widetilde\pi(\tilde q)\) and second coordinate \(d\widetilde\pi_{\tilde q}(\mathcal{O}^{\widetilde M}_{\tilde q})\), so \(\varphi'(\tilde q) = \varphi(\tilde q)\) for every \(\tilde q\).

A little covering-space theory now turns the connectedness criterion into a purely group-theoretic sufficient condition for orientability. Recall that the index of a subgroup is the number of its cosets.

Theorem (Simply Connected Manifolds Are Orientable)

Let \(M\) be a connected smooth manifold, with or without boundary, and suppose the fundamental group of \(M\) has no subgroup of index two. Then \(M\) is orientable. In particular, if \(M\) is simply connected, then it is orientable.

Proof.

A connected \(0\)-manifold is a single point, which is orientable, so we may assume that \(M\) has positive dimension. Suppose \(M\) is not orientable. By the Orientation Covering Theorem, its orientation covering \(\widehat\pi : \widehat M \to M\) is then a two-sheeted smooth covering map, in particular a covering map with two sheets. Fix \(q \in \widehat M\) and put \(p = \widehat\pi(q)\). By the correspondence between sheets and cosets, the subgroup \(\widehat\pi_*\bigl(\pi_1(\widehat M, q)\bigr)\) has index two in \(\pi_1(M, p)\). Thus nonorientability produces a subgroup of index two. Contrapositively, if the fundamental group of \(M\) has no subgroup of index two, then \(M\) is orientable. A simply connected manifold has trivial fundamental group, whose only subgroup has index one, so it is orientable.

Orientability across the application domains

The index-two criterion settles several manifolds at once that recur as domains for learning on curved spaces. Spheres \(S^n\) with \(n \geq 2\) are simply connected, hence orientable, as are compact connected Lie groups that happen to be simply connected. Orientability there is not an assumption to be checked case by case but a consequence of topology.

The nonorientable case is equally consequential. When data lives on a nonorientable space such as the projective plane, the oriented double cover is the canonical orientable space on which orientation-dependent constructions become available, and its uniqueness means that this passage is forced rather than chosen. The volume form assembled earlier, transported to such a cover, supplies the invariant measure on which integration and averaging on these domains rely.
